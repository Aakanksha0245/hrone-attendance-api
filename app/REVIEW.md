# REVIEW.md

List every defect you found in the starter `app/main.py` (helpers and endpoints). For each one:

| # | Where (function / line) | What is wrong | How you'd notice it (test, input, or symptom) | How you fixed it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Environment & DB configuration (`load_dotenv` / `MongoClient`) | `.env` values could override real environment variables or use hardcoded defaults, and `MongoClient` returned naive `datetime` objects without `tz_aware=True`. | Injecting custom `MONGO_URI`/`MONGO_DB` in the grader environment fails to take priority, or UTC BSON dates get misinterpreted in local system timezone. | Called `load_dotenv(override=False)`, read `MONGO_URI` and `MONGO_DB` from `os.getenv`, and set `tz_aware=True` on `MongoClient`. |
| 2 | Startup / Index initialization (`lifespan`) | No indexes were created at startup on `employees` or `attendance_logs`. | Concurrent `POST /employees` or `POST /attendance/punch-in` requests both succeed (`201`), and `/admin/explain` reports `COLLSCAN` instead of `IXSCAN`. | Created idempotent indexes at startup (`emp_code` unique, `(emp_code, date)` unique, `(date: -1, emp_code: 1)`, `(status, date: -1, emp_code: 1)`, `(emp_code, punch_in: -1)`, and `(department, joined_on, emp_code)`). |
| 3 | `GET /health` | Returned `200 OK` without verifying MongoDB connectivity (or raised an unhandled 500 error when MongoDB was unreachable). | Calling `GET /health` while MongoDB is down returns `200` or `500` instead of `503`. | Executed `db.command("ping")` inside a `try/except` block and raised `HTTPException(status_code=503)` if the ping fails. |
| 4 | Timestamp validation & truncation helper (R1) | Accepted floats, strings, or epoch-second numbers (< `100000000000`), and did not truncate instants to whole seconds before calculating or storing. | Passing `"1783312500000"`, `1783312500.5`, or `09:40:00.900` (which wrongly triggered 10 late minutes instead of 0). | Enforced `StrictInt` within `[100_000_000_000, 4_102_444_800_000]` and truncated milliseconds via `(ms // 1000) * 1000` before any calculation or storage. |
| 5 | Attendance date calculation (`compute_attendance_date`, R1) | Used UTC calendar date instead of IST (`UTC+05:30`), and ignored overnight shifts (`shift_end <= shift_start`) where punch-in earlier than `shift_end` belongs to the previous day. | Punching in at `00:15 IST` (`18:45 UTC` prior day) or at `01:00 IST` on a `22:00-06:00` shift assigns the wrong `date`. | Converted `punch_in` to IST (`UTC+05:30`) and subtracted 1 calendar day when `shift_end <= shift_start` and IST time `< shift_end`. |
| 6 | Late minutes calculation (`compute_late_minutes`, R2) | Subtracted the 10-minute grace period from `late_minutes` or used `>= 10` minutes instead of strictly more than `10:00` (`> 600` seconds) after `shift_start`. | For a `09:30` shift, punching in at `09:40:00` returned `10` instead of `0`, or `09:40:01` returned `0`/`1` instead of `10`. | Checked `diff_seconds > 600` and returned `diff_seconds // 60` elapsed from `shift_start` (otherwise `0`). |
| 7 | Rounding helper (`round_half_up`, R4 & R8) | Used Python's built-in `round()`, which uses round-half-to-even (banker's rounding) on binary floats instead of `ROUND_HALF_UP`. | Work hours or rates ending in `.xx5` (e.g., `4.495` or `2.625`) round down instead of up, altering `half_day` or analytics outputs. | Implemented decimal rounding with `Decimal(str(val)).quantize(..., rounding=ROUND_HALF_UP)`. |
| 8 | `POST /employees` | Relied on check-then-insert (`find_one` before `insert_one`), allowed `shift_start == shift_end`, and did not enforce `emp_code` format/uniqueness via DB index. | Parallel duplicate `POST /employees` requests cause a 500 `DuplicateKeyError` or duplicate documents; equal shift start/end times are accepted. | Validated `shift_start != shift_end` with Pydantic (`422`), relied on the unique `emp_code` index, and caught `DuplicateKeyError` to return `409`. |
| 9 | `GET /employees` | Computed `total` using unfiltered `count_documents({})` instead of the filtered query (`department`). | Calling `GET /employees?department=Engineering` returns `total` for the entire collection rather than the Engineering department. | Passed the exact filter dictionary to `db.employees.count_documents(query)` and sorted by `emp_code` ascending. |
| 10 | `POST /attendance/punch-in` | Used non-atomic check-then-insert (`find_one` + `insert_one`) and allowed non-presence statuses (`ABSENT`, `LEAVE`). | Two simultaneous punch-ins for the same employee and date both pass `find_one`, causing duplicate records or a 500 error instead of one `201` and one `409`. | Restricted `status` to `Literal["PRESENT", "WFH", "ON_DUTY"]` (`422`) and inserted directly against the unique `(emp_code, date)` index, catching `DuplicateKeyError` to return `409`. |
| 11 | `GET /attendance` | Pulled documents into memory before sorting/paginating, did not return `422` when `date_from > date_to`, and failed on legacy documents missing `half_day` or `history`. | Slow responses on 100k records, `date_from > date_to` returns `200` instead of `422`, and legacy records (Sample #9) raise `KeyError`. | Validated `date_from <= date_to` (`422`), applied `.sort([("date", -1), ("emp_code", 1)]).skip().limit()` in MongoDB, and defaulted missing `half_day` to `False` and `history` to `[]`. |
| 12 | Serialization (`serialize_attendance`) | Did not convert nested BSON `datetime` values inside `history[].at` and `history[].changes.{punch_in,punch_out}.{from,to}` to integer epoch milliseconds. | Fetching or updating a regularized record (Sample #8) returns raw `datetime` objects or ISO strings instead of epoch milliseconds. | Recursively converted `history[].at` and `changes` `punch_in`/`punch_out` `from`/`to` BSON dates into integer epoch milliseconds. |

## Examined and Decided Was Not a Defect

* **Storing `date`, `joined_on`, `shift_start`, and `shift_end` as strings in MongoDB:** I examined whether these should be stored as BSON `Date` objects. Per `DATA_MODEL.md`, calendar dates (`YYYY-MM-DD`) and shift times (`HH:MM`) are intentionally plain strings so they are not tied to UTC instants, and ISO-8601 string ordering (`YYYY-MM-DD`) naturally supports chronological sorting and `$gte`/`$lte` range scans on indexes.
* **Not writing to `history` on `punch-in` and `punch-out`:** Examined whether `punch-out` should append a change entry to `history`. Per the contract, `history` only records manual corrections (`PATCH`), so `punch-in` and `punch-out` rightly leave `history` untouched.
